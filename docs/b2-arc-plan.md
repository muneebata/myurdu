# B2 Arc Plan — Levels 25–30 (DRAFT for review)

*Status: proposal only. Nothing built. Reviewed by: (pending Muneeb)*

The B1 arc (L19–L24) ended with the train-journey capstone. B2 is where learners stop
translating in their heads and start *living* in the language: longer sentences, formal
registers, and the grammar that native speakers use constantly but no app teaches.

## The six levels

**L25 · Jo–Wo — Relative clauses** (`jo log mehnat karte haiṉ, wo āge baṛhte haiṉ`)
The correlative pattern that unlocks complex sentences. Urdu doesn't say "the man who came" —
it says "which man came, that man…". Pure pattern drilling with proverbs as free ammunition
(half the KAHAWATEIN bank already uses jo–wo).

**L26 · Kiyā jātā hai — The passive & impersonal** (`yahāṉ Urdu bolī jātī hai`)
"It is said…", "chai is served…" — the register of signs, news, and polite distance.
Signboard tie-in with R6; every example verifiable against real public signage patterns.

**L27 · Shāyad ho — Doubt, hope, and presumption** (`shāyad wo ā jāe / wo ghar par hogā`)
Subjunctive + presumptive future. The grammar of politeness and speculation — how you
soften requests and guess without committing. Pairs naturally with dua/blessing formulas
already taught (Allāh shifā de from RP5).

**L28 · Banā, banāyā, banwāyā — Causatives**
The three-step ladder (do it / make it / have it done) — `silnā, sīnā, silwānā` straight
from the new darzi role-play (RP8). The most "Urdu-brained" grammar in the arc; English
has nothing like it, and heritage speakers half-know it already.

**L29 · Sher-e-Punjab — Izafat & the formal register**
The Persian glue (`-e-`) that runs through poetry, titles, and news: sher-e-Punjab,
wazīr-e-āzam, khud-e-tashkhīs. Unlocks the Kutub Khana properly — learners finally
parse Ghalib's titles instead of memorizing them. Compound postpositions
(ke bāwajūd, ke zariye) round out formal prose.

**L30 · Dāwat — The dinner-party capstone**
A full evening as a mehmān: arriving, complimenting the khānā, refusing seconds three
times before accepting (takalluf!), telling a story at the table, taking leave properly.
Every grammar point from L25–29 appears naturally. Ties to R10's dāwat reader.
