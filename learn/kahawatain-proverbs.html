Kahawatain · Proverbs

The one-liners every aunty has locked and loaded

Proverbs (kahāwateṉ) are Urdu's compressed wisdom, vivid little scenes starring camels, monkeys, and buffaloes that get deployed daily in every household. Drop one at the right moment and you'll earn more respect than a hundred grammatically perfect sentences. Tap each to hear it.

The phrases

نیکی کر دریا میں ڈالnekī kar, daryā meṉ ḍāl: Do a good deed and throw it in the river, do good expecting nothing back
ڈوبتے کو تنکے کا سہاراḍūbte ko tinke kā sahārā: To the drowning, even a straw is support, any hope helps in desperation
بندر کیا جانے ادرک کا سوادbandar kyā jāne adrak kā swād: What does a monkey know of ginger's taste, pearls before swine
جس کی لاٹھی اس کی بھینسjis kī lāṭhī, us kī bhaiṉs: Whoever holds the stick owns the buffalo, might makes right
اونٹ کے منہ میں زیرہūṉṭ ke muṉh meṉ zīrā: Cumin in a camel's mouth, a laughably small portion
کہاوتkahāwat: proverba whole saying with a whole lesson inside
محاورہmuhāwara: idiomshorter: a turn of phrase, not a complete thought
مثالmisāl: examplemisāl ke taur par = for example
نصیحتnasīhat: advice, counselwhat elders give, whether or not it was requested
سبقsabaq: lessonsabaq sīkhnā, to learn a lesson, usually the hard way
تجربہtajruba: experiencethe thing proverbs are compressed from
زبانzabān: tongue, languageone word for the organ and for the language it speaks
حکمتhikmat: wisdomalso the name for traditional medicine, a hakīm's craft

Good to know

✨ Timing is everything: a proverb lands after the situation is obvious to everyone. Cousin got a tiny slice of cake? “Ūṉṭ ke muṉh meṉ zīrā.” Room nods sagely.
✨ Notice L6's kyā inside the monkey proverb, 'bandar KYĀ jāne' (what would a monkey know). Your question words moonlight in rhetorical flourishes.
✨ Proverbs are also pronunciation gyms: ḍūbte opens with a retroflex ḍ, bhaiṉs ends on a nasal. Sound School's greatest hits in the wild.
✨ Urdu proverb-dropping is a competitive sport at family gatherings: the elder who lands the most apt kahāwat wins the room. There is no trophy; the trophy is the sage nodding.
✨ Many kahāwateṉ exist nearly word-for-word across Urdu, Hindi, and Punjabi, proof that the subcontinent's grandmothers have been running a shared wisdom network for centuries.
