Dāwat · The Capstone Evening

A whole evening as the mehmān, every B2 pattern at one table

The B2 capstone is an evening, not an exam. You arrive with a gift, smell the biryani, survive the takalluf, compliment the cook, and leave with grace plus a container of food. Every level of this arc shows up at the table: jo–wo, the passive of custom, hopeful subjunctives, banwāyā, and one perfectly deployed izāfat. Play RP9 after this and the whole evening is yours.

The phrases

آج ہم دعوت پر جا رہے ہیں۔āj ham dāwat par jā rahe haiṉ: Tonight we're going to a dawat.Dāwat, the P2 word: an invitation that means a feast.
تحفہ لے جانا اچھا سمجھا جاتا ہے۔tohfā le jānā achhā samjhā jātā hai: Bringing a gift is considered proper.L26's custom-passive, stating the etiquette. Tohfā from Saqafat.
جو بنے گا، وہ کھائیں گے۔jo bane gā, wo khāeṉ ge: Whatever's made, we'll eat.The guest's creed, in L25's echo.
خوشبو آ رہی ہے!khushbū ā rahī hai!: Something smells amazing!Khushbū = fragrance (khush + bū, happy-smell). Say it as you cross the doorway.
آپ نے اتنا کیوں بنوایا؟āp ne itnā kyūṉ banwāyā?: Why did you have SO much made?L28's banwāyā, in the protest every host loves to hear. Itnā = this much.
شاید ہم سب نہ کھا سکیں۔shāyad ham sab na khā sakeṉ: We may not manage it all.L27's doubtful subjunctive, said while reaching for more.
میزبان کا شکریہ ادا کریں۔mezbān kā shukriya adā kareṉ: Thank the host properly.Shukriya adā karnā = to render thanks. Mezbān from Tehzeeb; adā from the schoolmaster's couplet.
کھانے کے بعد چائے پی جاتی ہے۔khāne ke bād chāy pī jātī hai: After dinner, chai is drunk.L26 again — because after-dinner chai is not a choice, it is a law.
آئیے، آپ ہی کا انتظار تھا۔āiye, āp hī kā intizār thā: Come in — we were only waiting for you!Intizār = waiting. The hī makes YOU the only one who mattered.
تکلف مت کیجیے گا۔takalluf mat kījiye gā: Please don't stand on ceremony.C4's takalluf with L16's gentled -gā. Everyone says it; no one means it.
کھانا ٹھنڈا ہو جائے گا!khānā ṭhanḍā ho jāe gā!: The food will get cold!The dinner bell of every household, rung by every ammī.
آپ تو مہمان ہیں!āp to mehmān haiṉ!: But you're the guest!What you hear when you try to help clear the table. You will lose this argument.
اگلی دعوت ہماری طرف۔aglī dāwat hamārī taraf: The next dawat is on us.Taraf = side (from the wedding's dulhan kī taraf). The only correct way to say thank you.
جیسا میزبان، ویسی دعوت۔jaisā mezbān, waisī dāwat: As the host, so the feast.Jaisā–waisā, the last of L25's echo pairs, used as a compliment.
آپ کی مہمان نوازی لاجواب ہے۔āp kī mehmān-nawāzī lājawāb hai: Your hospitality is beyond reply.Mehmān-nawāzī from C4, lājawāb from the food unit. The capstone compliment.
پھر ملیں گے، انشاءاللہ۔phir mileṉ ge, inshāallāh: We'll meet again, God willing.Where L10's little goodbye ends up after twenty more levels: the same words, now carrying a whole evening.

Good to know

✨ Dāwat means both the invitation and the feast itself — accepting one commits you to the other, which tells you everything about how seriously the meal is meant.
✨ Guests are traditionally spoken of as a blessing — mehmān rahmat hote haiṉ, 'guests are a mercy' — which is why the takalluf dance exists: refusing once honours the host's generosity by making them insist.
✨ This level's sixteen lines quietly use every grammar point of the B2 arc: jo–wo, the custom-passive, the hopeful subjunctive, banwāyā's causative, and one izāfat hiding inside mehmān-nawāzī. If the dāwat feels easy, the arc worked.
