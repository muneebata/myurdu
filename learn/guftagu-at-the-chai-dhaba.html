Guftagu · At the Chai Dhaba

The capstone: a real conversation, start to finish

Graduation scene: a roadside chai dhaba. Every line below uses what you've already built, greetings (L1), questions (L6), numbers (L3), food (L5), time (L8). Play both sides out loud: order the chai, then be the chai-wala. When this flows, you're conversational.

The phrases

یہاں چائے اچھی ہے؟yahāṉ chāy achhī hai?: Is the tea good here?You, sitting down. L6's yahāṉ + L5's chāy.
جی ہاں، بہت مزیدار!jī hāṉ, bahut mazedār!: Oh yes, very delicious!Chai-wala, with justified confidence.
دو چائے دیجیےdo chāy dījiye: Two teas, pleaseDījiye = 'please give', the polite request ending. L3's do.
کتنے روپے؟kitne rupaye?: How many rupees?Rupaye, the currency. L5's kitne, now with money.
سو روپےsau rupaye: One hundred rupeesSau = 100. Your first big number.
یہ لیجیےyeh lījiye: Here you goLījiye = 'please take', dījiye's twin. Hand over the cash.
بہت شکریہbahut shukriya: Thank you very muchL1 + L5's bahut. Gratitude, upgraded.
کوئی بات نہیںkoī bāt nahīṉ: Don't mention it / no problemLiterally 'no matter at all', the universal de-escalator.
پھر ملیں گےphir mileṉ ge: We'll meet againThe warm goodbye between people who mean it.
اللہ حافظAllāh hāfiz: Goodbye (God protect you)Level 1's fun fact, now in your own mouth. Full circle.
اور کیا لیں گے؟aur kyā leṉ ge?: What else will you have?The dhaba-wala's follow-up, before you've finished the first cup.
چھوٹا یا بڑا؟chhoṭā yā baṛā?: Small or large?Yā = or. Two words that turn any pair into a choice.
ابھی لایاabhī lāyā: Coming right upLiterally 'brought just now'. The future, promised in the past tense.
چائے گرم ہے، دھیان سےchāy garam hai, dhyān se: The tea's hot, carefulDhyān se, the same words painted on the back of every truck.
ایک کپ چائےek kap chāy: one cup of teaKap borrowed straight from English, said with a short a.
دودھ پتی ہے؟dūdh pattī hai?: Do you have doodh-patti?Tea brewed in milk alone, no water. The strong stuff.

Good to know

✨ Dījiye (please give) and lījiye (please take) are the -iye ending doing its magic: it turns any verb stem into a gracious request. Boliye (please speak), baiṭhiye (please sit), khāiye (please eat), instant courtesy.
✨ Koī bāt nahīṉ is the load-bearing phrase of desi politeness: it answers apologies, thanks, and minor disasters alike. Master it and you can absorb almost any social situation.
✨ A dhaba is a roadside eatery, truck-driver canteens that became beloved institutions. The chai is strong, the charpais are creaky, and the conversation is exactly the one you just learned.
