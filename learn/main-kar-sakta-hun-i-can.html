Main Kar Saktā Hūṉ · I Can

The power word saknā, say what you can (and can't) do

One little verb unlocks a hundred sentences. Saknā (to be able) rides on the stem of any verb you already know: bol saktā hūṉ, ā saktī hūṉ, kar sakte haiṉ. Learn the pattern once, apply it to everything.

The phrases

میں اردو بول سکتا ہوںmaiṉ urdū bol saktā hūṉ: I can speak UrduThe proudest sentence on this site. Women say bol saktī hūṉ.
کیا آپ مدد کر سکتے ہیں؟kyā āp madad kar sakte haiṉ?: Can you help?Madad = help. The polite āp always takes sakte haiṉ.
میں کل نہیں آ سکتاmaiṉ kal nahīṉ ā saktā: I can't come tomorrowNahīṉ slots in before the verb pair. L8's kal doing tomorrow-duty.
ہم کل مل سکتے ہیںhum kal mil sakte haiṉ: We can meet tomorrowMilnā = to meet, from L2's khushī huī family.
آپ یہاں بیٹھ سکتے ہیںāp yahāṉ baiṭh sakte haiṉ: You can sit hereOffer a seat like a host. Baiṭhnā = to sit.
کیا میں اندر آ سکتا ہوں؟kyā maiṉ andar ā saktā hūṉ?: May I come in?Andar = inside. L6's kyā turns ability into permission.
وہ اچھا گا سکتی ہےwoh achhā gā saktī hai: She can sing wellGānā = to sing; saktī agrees with her, not you.
آپ یہ کر سکتے ہیںāp yeh kar sakte haiṉ: You can do thisThe pep-talk sentence. Say it to the mirror before role-play.
جی ہاں، بالکلjī hāṉ, bilkul: Yes, absolutelyBilkul is the wholehearted yes: completely, totally, of course.
معاف کیجیے، میں نہیں کر سکتاmuāf kījiye, maiṉ nahīṉ kar saktā: Sorry, I can'tMuāf kījiye = forgive me. A woman says nahīṉ kar saktī.
کیوں نہیں!kyūṉ nahīṉ!: Why not! (of course)A question shaped like a refusal that means the opposite.
ایک منٹ، میں دیکھتا ہوںek minaṭ, maiṉ dekhtā hūṉ: One minute, let me seeDekhnā is to see, and also to check or look into something.
مجھے تھوڑی اردو آتی ہےmujhe thoṛī urdū ātī hai: I know a little UrduLanguages are not learned in Urdu, they COME to you: urdū ātī hai.
کیا آپ آہستہ بول سکتے ہیں؟kyā āp āhista bol sakte haiṉ?: Could you speak slowly?The single most useful sentence a learner owns.
میں سمجھ سکتا ہوں لیکن بول نہیں سکتاmaiṉ samajh saktā hūṉ lekin bol nahīṉ saktā: I can understand but I can't speakThe diaspora sentence. Lekin = but.
مجھ سے نہیں ہو گاmujh se nahīṉ ho gā: I won't manage itLiterally 'from me it will not happen'. The honest, humble no.

Good to know

✨ Saknā never stands alone, it always leans on another verb's bare stem: bol saktā, ā saktī, kar sakte. Steal the stem, add your power word.
✨ The ending carries the person: saktā (he/I-male), saktī (she/I-female), sakte (we/you-polite). The verb stem itself never changes.
✨ In fast speech 'nahīṉ ā saktā' often shrinks to 'nahīṉ ā saktā yār', the yār at the end softens the refusal between friends.
