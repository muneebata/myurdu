Chāhiye! · Wants & Needs

Need it, want it, should do it, the chāhiye family

Urdu doesn't say 'I want tea', it says 'to-me tea is-wanted': mujhe chāy chāhiye. Flip your English head around once and the whole family opens up: needs, wants, and gentle shoulds.

The phrases

مجھے چائے چاہیےmujhe chāy chāhiye: I need teaThe national sentence. Mujhe = to me, the wanting flows toward you.
آپ کو کیا چاہیے؟āp ko kyā chāhiye?: What do you need?Āp ko = to you. Shopkeepers say this all day.
مجھے پانی چاہیےmujhe pānī chāhiye: I need waterL3's pānī. Chāhiye never changes, it's frozen forever.
میں اردو سیکھنا چاہتا ہوںmaiṉ urdū sīkhnā chāhtā hūṉ: I want to learn UrduChāhnā = to want (an action). Women say chāhtī hūṉ.
وہ گھر جانا چاہتی ہےwoh ghar jānā chāhtī hai: She wants to go homeFull infinitive jānā + chāhnā, want-to-do sentences.
کیا آپ کچھ کھانا چاہتے ہیں؟kyā āp kuchh khānā chāhte haiṉ?: Would you like to eat something?Kuchh = something. The host's eternal question, expect nahīṉ, phir haan.
مجھے آرام کرنا چاہیےmujhe ārām karnā chāhiye: I should restInfinitive + chāhiye = should. Ārām = rest, as in ārām se (easy does it).
ہمیں جلدی کرنی چاہیےhameṉ jaldī karnī chāhiye: We should hurryHameṉ = to us. Jaldī from the bazaar, now with a deadline.
جی، ابھی لاتا ہوںjī, abhī lātā hūṉ: Yes, bringing it right awayAbhī = right now. Lānā = to bring.
اور کچھ چاہیے؟aur kuchh chāhiye?: Anything else?Every waiter, every shopkeeper, every ammī.
نہیں، بس شکریہnahīṉ, bas shukriya: No, that's all, thank youBas = enough, that's it. One of Urdu's hardest-working words.
کیا میں مدد کروں؟kyā maiṉ madad karūṉ?: Shall I help?Karūṉ, not kartā hūṉ: the shall-I form, for offers.
مجھے کچھ نہیں چاہیےmujhe kuchh nahīṉ chāhiye: I don't need anythingKuchh nahīṉ = nothing at all.
ہمیں دو کمرے چاہئیںhameṉ do kamre chāhieṉ: We need two roomsWanting more than one thing? chāhiye becomes chāhieṉ.
آپ کو آرام کرنا چاہیےāp ko ārām karnā chāhiye: You should restThe same chāhiye, now meaning 'should'. Advice, gently given.
مجھے جانا ہےmujhe jānā hai: I have to goVerb + hai = must. Heavier than chāhiye's should.

Good to know

✨ Chāhiye is grammatically frozen, one form for everyone, everything, always. It's the easiest 'verb' in the language because it refuses to change.
✨ The mujhe/āp ko pattern (dative subjects) runs deep in Urdu: hunger, thirst, liking, needing, all of them happen to you rather than by you.
✨ Chāhnā (to want) and chāhiye (is wanted/needed) are cousins from the same root, as is chāhat, the word for longing that fills half of Urdu poetry.
