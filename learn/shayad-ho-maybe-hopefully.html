Shāyad Ho · Maybe, Hopefully

Doubt, hope, and educated guessing, the grammar of politeness

Drop the -tā and the verb stops stating and starts hoping: wo ātā hai (he comes) becomes shāyad wo āe (maybe he'll come). And hogā turns any sentence into a good guess: wo ghar par hogā, he'll be home, probably. This level is how Urdu stays polite — nothing is demanded, everything is wished, presumed, or left to Allāh.

The phrases

شاید وہ آ جائے۔shāyad wo ā jāe: Maybe he'll come.Āe, not ātā hai — the subjunctive drops certainty along with the -tā.
شاید بارش ہو۔shāyad bārish ho: It might rain.Ho, the maybe-form of hai. Your L13 word shāyad, now with its own grammar.
وہ گھر پر ہوگا۔wo ghar par hogā: He's probably home.The presumptive: future form, present guess. Said while knocking.
آپ تھک گئے ہوں گے۔āp thak gaye hoṉ ge: You must be tired.What every host says as you arrive. A guess offered as care.
ہو سکتا ہے۔ho saktā hai: Could be. It's possible.Saknā on honā itself. Two words, endlessly useful.
اللہ کرے سب ٹھیک ہو۔Allāh kare sab ṭhīk ho: God grant that all is well.Kare and ho, both subjunctive — a whole sentence of wishing.
کاش میں وہاں ہوتا۔kāsh maiṉ wahāṉ hotā: If only I were there.Kāsh = if only, the wistful particle. Hotā = the unreal past, the were of regret.
دعا کریں کہ کام ہو جائے۔duā kareṉ ke kām ho jāe: Pray that it works out.Duā from Iqbal's poem, ke from L26, ho jāe from this very level.
انشاءاللہ ہو جائے گا۔inshāallāh ho jāe gā: God willing, it'll get done.The eternal answer of every electrician, tailor and cousin. A promise and a prayer at once.
دیکھتے ہیں کیا ہوتا ہے۔dekhte haiṉ kyā hotā hai: Let's see what happens.L22's dekhte haiṉ, now with its full clause.
ایسا نہ ہو کہ دیر ہو جائے۔aisā na ho ke der ho jāe: Let it not be that we run late.The worry-clause: na + subjunctive. How Urdu says 'lest'.
کوئی بات ہوگی۔koī bāt hogī: There must be some reason.The charitable guess. Hogī presumes without accusing.
جیسے بھی ہو، آ جائیں۔jaise bhī ho, ā jāeṉ: However it works out, do come.Jaise bhī = by whatever means. An invitation with the obstacles pre-forgiven.
اگر ہو سکے تو بتا دیں۔agar ho sake to batā deṉ: If it's possible, let me know.Ho sake — saknā itself goes subjunctive. Requests don't get softer than this.
شاید ہی کوئی ہو جو چائے نہ پیے۔shāyad hī koī ho jo chāy na piye: There's hardly a soul who doesn't drink chai.Shāyad hī flips shāyad to 'hardly'. And there's L25's jo, holding the clause.
اللہ جانے۔Allāh jāne: God knows.The subcontinent's shrug. Jāne — even knowing goes subjunctive here.

Good to know

✨ The presumptive is a real tense: wo ghar par hogā uses the future form to make a present guess. English does the same with 'that will be the postman' — Urdu just does it constantly.
✨ Kāsh came from Persian (kāsh ke), and it powers a whole genre: the ghazal of regret. When you can hear kāsh coming, you know the couplet ends in heartbreak.
✨ Politeness in Urdu is grammatical, not just lexical: requests arrive as subjunctives (ho sake to…), guesses as presumptives (hoṉ ge), hopes as duās. The mood system IS the manners system.
