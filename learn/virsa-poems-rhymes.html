The phrases

لب پہ آتی ہے دعا بن کے تمنا میریlab pe ātī hai duā ban ke tamannā merī: My longing comes to my lips, transformed into a prayer
زندگی شمع کی صورت ہو خدایا میریzindagī shamma kī sūrat ho khudāyā merī: O God, may my life be like a candle (giving light to others)
مچھلی جل کی رانی ہےmachhlī jal kī rānī hai: The fish is the queen of the water
جیون اس کا پانی ہےjīwan us kā pānī hai: Water is her life
ہزاروں خواہشیں ایسی کہ ہر خواہش پہ دم نکلےhazāroṉ khwāhisheṉ aisī ke har khwāhish pe dam nikle: Thousands of desires, each worth dying for…
بہت نکلے مرے ارمان لیکن پھر بھی کم نکلےbahut nikle mere armān lekin phir bhī kam nikle: …many of mine were fulfilled, yet somehow too few

Good to know

✨ Dil Dil Pakistan (Vital Signs, 1987), often called Pakistan's 'second national anthem'. A pop song so beloved it's practically a state institution.
✨ Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan: the qawwali legend. Start with 'Allah Hoo' or 'Tumhein Dillagi'; you'll hear Urdu and Persian devotional poetry at full power.
✨ Coke Studio Pakistan: the modern gateway drug to Urdu music. 'Pasoori' (2022) became a global hit. Punjabi-Urdu blend with subtitles everywhere.
✨ Faiz Ahmed Faiz: the 20th century's revolutionary romantic. His 'Hum Dekhenge' is recited at protests across South Asia to this day.
✨ The ghazal. Urdu's signature poetic form, is a chain of self-contained couplets, each ending on the same refrain. At a mushā'ira (poetry gathering), the audience shouts 'wāh wāh!' when a couplet lands. Audience participation is mandatory.
✨ Iqbal's 'Lab pe aati hai dua' and Ghalib's couplets are public-domain treasures. Urdu's canon is older than copyright, free for every learner.
