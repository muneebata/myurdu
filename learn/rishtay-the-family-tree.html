Rishtay · The Family Tree

four aunts, four uncles, and why the words matter

English has one word for aunt. Urdu has four, and which one you use announces exactly how she is related to you. This is the unit for everyone who has frozen at a shaadi trying to work out khālā or phūphī. The rule underneath is simple: abbū's side and ammī's side get different words, always. Learn the map once and you will never be corrected at a dawat again.

The phrases

تائیtāyī: tāyā's wifeaunt by marriage, abbū's side
چچیchachī: chachā's wifeaunt by marriage, abbū's side
پھوپھاphūphā: phūphī's husbanduncle by marriage, abbū's side
مامیmāmī: māmūṉ's wifeaunt by marriage, ammī's side
خالوkhālū: khālā's husbanduncle by marriage, ammī's side
ساسsās: mother-in-lawthe most powerful character in every drama
سسرsasur: father-in-lawsās and sasur, always a pair
بہوbahū: daughter-in-lawthe sās-bahū saga is a whole TV genre
دامادdāmād: son-in-lawthe honored guest at every dawat
سالاsālā: wife's brothercareful: also a rude word outside the family
سالیsālī: wife's sisterthe shaadi-season tease
دیورdevar: husband's younger brotherthe ally in the household
جیٹھjeṭh: husband's elder brotherwith the retroflex ṭh
نندnand: husband's sistera famous frenemy of drama plots
آپاāpā: elder sister (as a name)what you call her, not just what she is
باجیbājī: elder sister (as a name)same job as āpā, house by house
بھائی جانbhāī-jān: dear elder brother-jān turns any rishta affectionate

Good to know

✨ Urdu has no everyday word for cousin. Formally your chachā's son is your chachā-zād bhāī ('chachā-born brother'), but in speech cousins are simply bhāī and behen, family, not a separate category. The English word 'cousin' gets borrowed when precision is needed.
✨ The grandparent mnemonic: abbū's side both start with d (dādā, dādī), ammī's side both start with n (nānā, nānī). Two letters, and you will never mix them up again.
✨ Mixing up khālā and phūphī is the classic diaspora mistake, and every aunty notices, because the words say which side of the family she belongs to. Get it right once at a dawat and watch the delight.
