Awaazain · The Urdu Mouth

Rolled Rs, curled Ts, puffs of air — train the tricky sounds

Ten minutes here pays off everywhere else. Urdu has a handful of sounds English simply doesn't use — but your mouth already knows most of them from other words. Each section shows you where the sound is made, tells you exactly what to do with your tongue, then gives you words to tap, hear, and echo.

Good to know

✨ Urdu's r is not the English growl. It's a single quick tap of the tongue-tip against the ridge behind your top teeth.
✨ You already make this sound! Say “butter” or “water” fast, American-style — that flappy 'tt' in the middle IS the Urdu ر. Now put it where an r goes.
✨ Want a full rolling trill? Relax the tongue-tip, hold it loosely at the ridge, and push air steadily so it flutters — like a cat's purr. Practice with 'tr' words: say “butter-up” faster and faster until the tap buzzes.
✨ These are the sounds that make Urdu sound like Urdu. Curl your tongue-tip up and back to the roof of your mouth, then flick it forward as you release.
✨ English t/d are halfway there already — just exaggerate the curl. Urdu ears hear English 't' as ٹ, which is why 'Tom' gets written ٹام.
✨ ڑ (ṛe) is the boss level: a retroflex flap — curl back, then tap while uncurling. It never starts a word, so you always have a running start.
