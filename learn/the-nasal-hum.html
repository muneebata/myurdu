The Nasal Hum · ں

Send the sound through your nose

For ں the little curtain at the back of your mouth lowers and the sound floats out through your nose, the hum that ends words like nahīṉ.

Good to know

✨ The dotless nūn (ں) means: send the vowel through your nose and never let the tongue touch. Like the French “bon”, there's no real 'n' at the end, just a hum.
✨ It's everywhere in the words you already know: hāṉ (yes), nahīṉ (no), maiṉ (I). Say “huh” while humming through your nose, that's hāṉ.
✨ The dotless ں only turns up at the end of a syllable. In the middle of a word the same nasal hum is written with an ordinary ن, so dāṉt (tooth) hides it in plain sight and you simply have to know it is there.
✨ French, Portuguese and Punjabi nasalise vowels exactly this way, so anyone who can say 'bon' already owns the muscle. English dropped the habit centuries ago and now only hums through the nose by accident.
