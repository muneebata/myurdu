Mulaqat · Introductions

Say who you are, ask who they are

Now that you can greet (Level 1), let's exchange names and origins. You'll also meet Urdu's famous three levels of 'you', politeness is built right into the grammar.

The phrases

میرا نام مونیب ہےmerā nām Muneeb hai: My name is MuneebSwap in your own name. 'Merā' = my, 'hai' = is.
آپ کا نام کیا ہے؟āp kā nām kyā hai?: What is your name?'Āp' is the respectful 'you', your default with strangers.
آپ سے مل کر خوشی ہوئیāp se mil kar khushī huī: Pleased to meet youLiterally 'meeting you, happiness happened'.
میں امریکہ سے ہوںmaiṉ Amrīkā se hūṉ: I am from AmericaPattern: maiṉ ___ se hūṉ = I'm from ___.
آپ کہاں سے ہیں؟āp kahāṉ se haiṉ?: Where are you from?'Kahāṉ' = where. Notice the nasal ṉ, it's everywhere in Urdu.
میں اردو سیکھ رہا ہوںmaiṉ urdū sīkh rahā hūṉ: I am learning Urdu (male speaker)Verbs agree with the speaker's gender: rahā (m).
میں اردو سیکھ رہی ہوںmaiṉ urdū sīkh rahī hūṉ: I am learning Urdu (female speaker)…and rahī (f). Same sentence, different ending.
آہستہ بولیےāhista boliye: Please speak slowlyThe learner's best friend. Use it without shame.

Good to know

✨ Urdu has three words for “you”: tū (intimate/blunt), tum (casual, friends), and āp (respectful). Getting them wrong can be as awkward as calling your boss “dude”, when in doubt, use āp.
✨ Urdu sentences run Subject–Object–Verb: “maiṉ urdū sīkh rahā hūṉ” is literally “I Urdu learning am.” The verb always parks at the end.
✨ Spoken Urdu and spoken Hindi are largely mutually intelligible, learn one and you can chat with well over half a billion people.
