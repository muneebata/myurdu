Kaam · Essential Verbs

Eat, drink, go, come, the engines of every sentence

Corpus research says a handful of verbs power most daily speech. Here are Urdu's core eight, plus the sentence pattern that runs them. Remember the golden rule from Level 2: the verb always parks at the END.

The phrases

کھاناkhānā: to eatYes, the same word as 'food' from Level 5. Context decides.
پیناpīnā: to drink
جاناjānā: to go
آناānā: to come
دیکھناdekhnā: to see / watch'Dekho!', look! You'll hear it constantly.
کرناkarnā: to doThe workhorse: pairs with nouns to make new verbs, kām karnā (to work).
بولناbolnā: to speakFrom Level 2's 'āhista boliye', now you own the verb itself.
سمجھناsamajhnā: to understand
میں بازار جا رہا ہوںmaiṉ bāzār jā rahā hūṉ: I am going to the bazaarSOV in action: 'I bazaar going am.' Verb last, always.
مجھے سمجھ نہیں آیاmujhe samajh nahīṉ āyā: I didn't understandThe honest learner's best sentence. Deploy freely.

Good to know

✨ Every Urdu infinitive ends in -nā: khānā, pīnā, jānā, ānā. Spot a -nā, you've spotted a verb. English wishes it were this organised.
✨ Karnā (to do) is Urdu's LEGO brick: kām karnā (work-do = to work), fon karnā (phone-do = to call), maaf karnā (forgive-do = to forgive). Learn one verb, unlock dozens.
✨ Urdu word order is basically Yoda's: “maiṉ chāy pī rahā hūṉ” = “I tea drinking am.” Embrace the inner Yoda and the grammar clicks.
