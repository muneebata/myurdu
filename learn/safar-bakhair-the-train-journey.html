Safar Bakhair · The Train Journey

The B1 capstone, buy the ticket, find the seat, ride the rails

Pakistan runs on its railways, and this capstone runs on everything you've learned: can-do saknā, polite chāhiye, comparisons at the ticket window, compound verbs in the carriage. One journey, Karachi to Lahore, entirely in Urdu.

The phrases

ایک ٹکٹ لاہور کاek ṭikaṭ lāhaur kā: One ticket to LahoreṬikaṭ, another loanword travelling both directions. Point, say, pay.
ٹرین کب آئے گی؟ṭren kab āe gī?: When will the train come?L15's future + L6's kab. Ṭren is feminine, āe gī.
کتنا وقت لگے گا؟kitnā waqt lage gā?: How long will it take?Lagnā = to take (time). The traveler's most-used verb.
کیا یہ سیٹ خالی ہے؟kyā yeh sīṭ khālī hai?: Is this seat free?Khālī = empty. Ask before you settle, carriage etiquette.
میں کھڑکی کے پاس بیٹھنا چاہتا ہوںmaiṉ khiṛkī ke pās baiṭhnā chāhtā hūṉ: I want to sit by the windowL20's chāhnā + ke pās (near, from RP4). Khiṛkī = window.
اگلا اسٹیشن کون سا ہے؟aglā isṭeshan kaun sā hai?: What's the next station?Aglā = next. Kaun sā = which one. L6's kaun, upgraded.
ہم وقت پر پہنچ جائیں گےhum waqt par pahuṉch jāeṉ ge: We'll arrive on timePahuṉchnā + jānā. L23's completion flavor, applied to arrival. Inshāallah understood.
سفر بخیر!safar bakhair!: Safe travels!The blessing for every departure. Say it to fellow passengers and mean it.
ٹرین دیر سے ہےṭren der se hai: The train is lateDer = lateness. The announcement you will hear most.
پلیٹ فارم نمبر دوpleṭfārm nambar do: Platform number twoBorrowed whole, said with Urdu vowels.
ٹکٹ دکھائیےṭikaṭ dikhāiye: Show your ticket, pleaseDikhānā = to show, with L16's polite -iye.
یہ سیٹ میری ہےyeh sīṭ merī hai: This seat is mineSīṭ behaves as feminine here, so merī, not merā.
سامان کہاں رکھوں؟sāmān kahāṉ rakhūṉ?: Where should I put my luggage?Sāmān = luggage, goods, stuff. Rakhūṉ = shall I put.
کھڑکی کھول دیں؟khiṛkī khol deṉ?: Shall I open the window?L23's khol denā, doing someone else the favour.
چائے والا آ رہا ہےchāy wālā ā rahā hai: The chai seller is comingThe best sentence on any Pakistani train.
آپ کا سفر اچھا گزرےāp kā safar achhā guzre: May your journey go wellGuzre = may it pass. A blessing, not a statement.

Good to know

✨ Pakistan's railways began with the Scinde Railway's Karachi–Kotri line, opened in 1861, among the oldest in South Asia. The Karachi-to-Peshawar main line still spans the whole country.
✨ The line to Quetta climbs the Bolan Pass on British-era bridges and tunnels cut straight through the cliffs, and trains still run it. Safar bakhair means something on that stretch.
✨ Safar means journey, and yes, English 'safari' comes from the same Arabic root, having traveled through Swahili on the way. A journey word that journeyed.
✨ Train, ticket, station, seat. Urdu absorbed the railway's English vocabulary and stamped it with its own pronunciation: ṭren, ṭikaṭ, isṭeshan, sīṭ. Say them the Urdu way and you're instantly more local.
