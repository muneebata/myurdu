Tareekh · History

5,000 years: Indus cities to Independence

The land that is now Pakistan has hosted one of humanity's first great civilizations, Buddhist universities, Sufi saints, Mughal splendor, and the birth of a nation — all along the same river.

Good to know

✨ ~2500 BCE — Mohenjo-daro and Harappa. Grid streets, standardized bricks, covered drains, public baths — indoor plumbing millennia before Rome. At its peak the Indus civilization was larger than Egypt and Mesopotamia combined.
✨ Its script is still undeciphered — thousands of inscribed seals, no Rosetta Stone. One of archaeology's great unsolved puzzles is sitting in Sindh.
✨ Want to go deeper? Harappa.com has hosted decades of Indus Valley scholarship, excavation slides, and essays by the archaeologists themselves — the best rabbit hole on the ancient Indus anywhere online.
✨ The famous bronze Dancing Girl statuette of Mohenjo-daro — four inches of pure attitude, hand on hip — is 4,500 years old.
✨ Around Taxila and Peshawar (roughly 500 BCE–500 CE), the Gandhara civilization flourished at the crossroads of the Silk Road.
✨ Taxila hosted one of the ancient world's great centers of learning — students traveled from across Asia to study medicine, law, and archery there.
