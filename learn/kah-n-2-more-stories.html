Two more little stories, an invitation, and bazaar bargaining

The phrases

میرے دوست نے کہا: کل گھر آؤmere dost ne kahā: kal ghar āo: My friend said: come over tomorrow.kahā + the L16 tum-command āo
میں نے کہا: ٹھیک ہے، ضرورmaiṉ ne kahā: ṭhīk hai, zarūr: I said: okay, definitely.zarūr, your R9 word, back already
کل ہم ملیں گےkal ham mileṉ ge: Tomorrow we will meet.kal + future verb = tomorrow (the L8 trick)
ہم چائے پئیں گےham chāy pīeṉge: We will drink chai.of course you will
دوست کا گھر اچھا ہےdost kā ghar achhā hai: My friend's house is nice.kā possession, house OF friend
بہت اچھا دن ہو گاbahut achhā din ho gā: It will be a very good day.ho gā, will be
میں بازار گیاmaiṉ bāzār gayā: I went to the bazaar.the L14 line you know by heart
آم کتنے کے ہیں؟ām kitne ke haiṉ?: How much are the mangoes?kitne ke, asking a plural price
وہ بہت مہنگے تھےwoh bahut mahange the: They were very expensive.NEW BEND: mahangā flexes to mahange for plural
میں نے کہا: تھوڑا سستا کریںmaiṉ ne kahā: thoṛā sastā kareṉ: I said: make it a little cheaper.the sacred bazaar phrase, straight from L11
ہم نے دس آم لیےham ne das ām liye: We took ten mangoes.NEW BEND: lenā becomes liye in the ne-past
امی بہت خوش ہوئیںammī bahut khush huīṉ: Mom was very happy.huīṉ, the respectful plural for elders

Good to know

✨ Mol-tol (haggling) has rules of courtesy: the seller quotes high knowing you'll counter, and walking away slowly is a legitimate negotiating move, the call-back discount is real.
✨ Mangoes are sold by the darjan (dozen) or the kilo depending on the city, and a practiced ammī can judge a whole crate's ripeness from the smell alone.
