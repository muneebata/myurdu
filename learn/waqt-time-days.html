Waqt · Time & Days

Today, tomorrow, and the word that means both

Time words let you make plans, and Urdu's are famously efficient: one word covers both yesterday AND tomorrow, and context does the rest. Combine these with Level 7 verbs: 'maiṉ kal ā rahā hūṉ'. I'm coming tomorrow.

The phrases

آجāj: today
کلkal: yesterday AND tomorrowOne word, both directions, the verb tense tells you which. See the fun fact.
پرسوںparsoṉ: two days away (either direction)Same trick as kal, one day further out.
صبحsubah: morning'Subah bakhair', good morning.
دوپہرdopahar: afternoonLiterally 'two watches', old-school timekeeping.
شامshām: evening
راتrāt: night'Shab bakhair', good night (shab is the poetic word for rāt).
ابھیabhī: right now'Abhī abhī' doubled = just this second.
بعد میںbaad meṉ: laterThe polite way to dodge anything.
ہفتہhafta: week (also Saturday)From Persian haft, seven, a week of seven days.
جمعہjumma: FridayThe week's big day, 'Jumma Mubarak!' greetings fly every Friday.
کتنے بجے ہیں؟kitne baje haiṉ?: What time is it?Bajnā is what a bell does. The hour is what has struck.
تین بجےtīn baje: at three o'clockNumber + baje. That is the whole clock system.
اگلے ہفتےagle hafte: next weekAglā = next, the same word as L24's aglā isṭeshan.
پچھلے سالpichhle sāl: last yearPichhlā = previous, behind. Sāl = year.
ہمیشہhamesha: alwaysAnd its opposite, kabhī nahīṉ, never.

Good to know

✨ Kal means yesterday OR tomorrow, the verb disambiguates: “kal maiṉ gayā” (yesterday I went) vs “kal maiṉ jāūṉgā” (tomorrow I'll go). Urdu treats time as distance from today, not direction. Parsoṉ does the same at two days.
✨ Dopahar (afternoon) literally means 'two pahars', a pahar is an ancient three-hour watch of the day. The old eight-watch clock still hides inside everyday words.
✨ Many Urdu day-names count in Persian: do-shamba, se-shamba… but Friday is jumma (from Arabic 'gathering'), the day everyone meets.
