Khana · Food & Requests

Order food, ask prices, praise the cook

The victory lap: everything comes together. Greetings (L1), polite requests (L2's āp), numbers for prices (L3) — now use them where it matters most: food.

The phrases

پانیpānī — water
چائےchāy — teaThe national obsession. Milky, sweet, constant.
کھاناkhānā — food / a mealAlso the verb 'to eat'. Context tells you which.
روٹیroṭī — flatbreadSo essential it can just mean 'a meal'.
مجھے پانی چاہیےmujhe pānī chāhiye — I need waterPattern: mujhe ___ chāhiye = I need/want ___.
یہ کیا ہے؟yeh kyā hai? — What is this?Point politely and learn every dish's name.
کتنے کا ہے؟kitne kā hai? — How much is it?Your Level 3 'kitne' earning its keep.
بہت مزیدارbahut mazedār — Very deliciousThe highest compliment to any cook.
بہت اچھاbahut achhā — Very good'Bahut' = very. Upgrade anything with it.
بس، شکریہbas, shukriya — That's enough, thank you'Bas' = enough/that's it. You'll need it — hospitality is relentless.

Good to know

✨ Saying “chai tea” is saying “tea tea” — chāy IS the word for tea. It travelled from Chinese chá along the overland trade routes; languages that got tea by sea (like English) say “tea” instead.
✨ “Bas” (enough) may be the most load-bearing word in desi hospitality. Hosts will refill your plate until you deploy it — and often twice more after that.
✨ Urdu is a champion borrower: pānī is Sanskrit-derived, chāy came via Persian from Chinese, mazedār is Persian, shukriya is Arabic. One sentence can span four civilizations.
