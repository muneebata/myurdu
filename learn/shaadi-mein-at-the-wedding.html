Shaadi Mein · At the Wedding

The grand capstone — survive and shine as a wedding guest

The final exam is a shaadi. Blessings, small talk with rishtedaar, the eternal food question, and a firm refusal to dance — every tense and trick from eighteen levels, deployed where it matters most. Play both sides out loud.

The phrases

شادی مبارک ہو!shādī mubārak ho! — Congratulations on the wedding!Your entrance line. L13's mubārak, upgraded to an occasion.
آپ دلہن کی طرف سے ہیں؟āp dulhan kī taraf se haiṉ? — Are you from the bride's side?Dulhan = bride. The shaadi ice-breaker.
میں دولہا کا دوست ہوںmaiṉ dūlhā kā dost hūṉ — I'm the groom's friendDūlhā = groom, dost from L4. Now you belong.
بہت خوبصورت شادی ہےbahut khūbsūrat shādī hai — It's a beautiful weddingKhūbsūrat from L12's weather exclamation — recycled for maximum aunty-approval.
مہندی کل تھیmehndī kal thī — The mehndi was yesterdayL14 past + P2's mehndī. Kal = yesterday here — you know why.
ولیمہ پرسوں ہوگاwalīmah parsoṉ hogā — The walima is the day after tomorrowFuture hogā + L8's parsoṉ. The three-event timeline, mastered.
کھانا کب ملے گا؟khānā kab milegā? — When will the food come?The true national anthem of wedding guests. L6 kab + future.
بریانی مزیدار ہےbiryānī mazedār hai — The biryani is deliciousSay it whether or not there's aloo. Peace matters.
ناچیے!nāchiye! — Dance, please!The -iye courtesy, weaponized by aunties.
میں نہیں ناچوں گاmaiṉ nahīṉ nāchūṉgā — I will NOT danceL16 negation + L15 future. A complete, doomed sentence.

Good to know

✨ “Khānā kab milegā?” is whispered at every shaadi on Earth roughly forty minutes after arrival. You now whisper it grammatically.
✨ The event sequence you can now narrate in three tenses: mehndī kal thī (past), shādī āj hai (present), walīmah parsoṉ hogā (future). Eighteen levels, one sentence family.
✨ Declaring maiṉ nahīṉ nāchūṉgā has never once prevented the dancing. The aunties always win. Consider it your final listening exercise.
